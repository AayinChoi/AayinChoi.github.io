Engineering contribution

The work moved from numbers to architecture.

The team first established the vehicle baseline. My responsibility was to make that concept close mathematically and structurally: identify a compatible propulsion system, verify its mass relationship to each stage, size critical load-bearing interfaces, and convert the results into a coherent vehicle layout. Once the design was fixed, I also created the visualization used to communicate it in the final poster.

01Milestone 4

Select the propulsion system—and prove it fits.

Using the stage geometry, structural masses, and mission conditions passed to me by the team, I independently evaluated engine options through repeated calculations. I selected a three-engine RS-27A cluster for Stage 1 and an AJ10-118K for Stage 2, then checked both propulsion systems against the vehicle’s structural mass and overall proportions.

02Structural verification

Size the interfaces before drawing the vehicle.

I used Aluminum 7075-T651 properties and the specified loading conditions to size the mounting and inter-stage bulkheads. Both applied-stress checks remained below the material yield strength, establishing defensible component dimensions for the final configuration rather than relying on visual proportion alone.

03Milestone 5

Convert the calculations into a complete architecture.

This was the most demanding phase of my individual contribution. Building from the Delta II 7000 baseline, I defined the two-stage geometry, overall dimensions, engine arrangement, payload section, materials, and major interfaces. The hand-drafted assembled, exploded, and engine-layout views kept every design decision traceable to the supporting calculations.

133 ft height 12 ft max diameter 3-engine cluster
04Technical communication

Make the finalized concept readable at a glance.

After the configuration was approved, I independently produced a generative-AI-assisted concept visualization to communicate the stacked vehicle more clearly than the original hand sketch. It was not used as an engineering model; it served as a presentation asset and was adopted by the team for the final project poster.
