Project context

A team project, viewed through the decisions I owned.

The complete course project included additional work completed across the four-person team. This page is not a milestone report. It uses a few selected artifacts to show how I approached the parts assigned to me, resolved the design challenges, and carried those decisions into the team’s final vehicle concept.

The documents shown below are excerpts—not the full milestones or the full project.

01First assignment

Make the inherited vehicle baseline viable.

A teammate handed me the rocket’s baseline conditions and data. From that starting point, I independently compared engine options, tested alternatives through repeated calculations, and identified a usable propulsion arrangement that fit the proposed vehicle.

The goal was not simply to name an engine. I had to verify that the choice remained credible when its mass and the vehicle’s proportions were considered together. That work became my contribution to Milestone 4.

InputShared vehicle requirements
ActionIndependent option study
OutcomeViable propulsion choice
02Most demanding phase

Turn separate calculations into one coherent vehicle.

For Milestone 5, I moved beyond a single component decision. Using existing launch vehicles as references, I independently developed the overall configuration while accounting for structure, materials, dimensions, component placement, and weight.

I translated those linked decisions into a complete hand-developed layout and supported the design with calculations rather than visual guesswork. This was the most time-intensive and physically demanding part of my contribution, but it produced a configuration the team could carry forward as one integrated vehicle.

Result A complete vehicle architecture with traceable engineering decisions.
03Self-initiated addition

Make the approved design easier to understand.

Once the configuration was finalized, I saw that the hand sketch communicated the engineering logic but was not the clearest presentation image. I independently created a cleaner, generative-image-assisted concept visual and provided it to the team.

The image was never presented as a CAD or analytical model. It was a visual communication tool derived from the approved configuration, and the team adopted it for the final poster.

Communication asset only · not a CAD or analysis model
